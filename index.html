If you've been scammed

Act quickly. Protect yourself first.

A report can document what happened, but it should not replace immediate steps with your bank, card issuer, exchange, platform or relevant authorities.

1Stop contact and paymentsDo not send additional money to “recover” funds or unlock an account.
2Secure affected accountsChange compromised passwords and enable stronger account security where appropriate.
3Contact the payment providerAsk your bank, card issuer or payment service what dispute or fraud options are available.
4Keep evidenceSave messages, receipts, transaction references, URLs and screenshots in a safe place.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an account to report a scam?

Yes. The supplied implementation uses Supabase Auth so a report is tied to the authenticated user account.

Can someone access my report if they know my reference number?

The supplied RPC lookup checks the authenticated user's identity and only returns a report belonging to that account.

What should I include in my description?

Explain who contacted you, how they contacted you, what they claimed, what you were asked to do, dates, amounts, payment methods and any relevant website or profile information.

Should I send passwords or recovery phrases?

No. Never submit passwords, recovery phrases, banking login credentials or other secrets.

Will reporting guarantee a refund?

No. Reporting documents the incident and supports review; it does not guarantee recovery of money or a particular outcome.

Is the evidence upload active?

The current frontend contains an evidence selector, but the supplied JavaScript does not upload that file. Private storage and a server-side signed-download flow still need to be configured before production use.
